The attack

How triangulation fraud works

  1. 1. Agent buys from a fake store

    Your customer's shopping agent finds a "too-good-to-be-true" deal on a fake storefront that looks just like the real thing. Same product photos, same branding, half the price.

  2. 2. Card details stolen

    The fake store harvests the shopper's card details at checkout. The shopper gets nothing, or a cheap counterfeit. The agent has no way to tell the store isn't real.

  3. 3. Fraudster buys from you

    Using the stolen card, the fraudster places a real order on your store. They ship it to the original buyer's address so the buyer never complains. To you, it looks like a normal order.

  4. 4. You eat the chargeback

    The card's real owner disputes the charge. You've shipped the goods, you can't recover them, and you lose the chargeback. The fraudster keeps the original payment.

The defence

Three defences, one verifier

Signed checkout

Your store publishes a key at your domain and signs every cart and price it sends to an agent. A fake storefront can copy the design and the product photos, but it can't sign as your domain. The agent's check fails, and it refuses to pay.

Open source — free for agents
{{icon:file-check}}
Recipient matching

For every agent order, ProveTheBuy compares who the order ships to with the shopper who signed the approval. If the approval is missing or comes from a different person than the recipient, it's a step-up. When the stolen card's owner disputes, you can show in seconds the order wasn't placed by them.

Indie plan and up

Cross-store pattern detection

In ProveTheBuy Cloud, hashed shipping addresses are compared across stores — no store sees another's customer data. The pattern: one address receiving orders paid by different cards, without matching signed approvals. That's the fingerprint of a triangulation operation.

For agent builders

Your agent can verify the store is real

Signed checkout isn't just for merchants. It gives your agent a way to verify it's buying from a real store before it pays. verifyMerchant() is free, open source, and takes three lines of code.

import { verifyMerchant } from "@provethebuy/verify";

const result = await verifyMerchant(signedCart, "nike.com");
if (!result.valid) throw new Error("Fake store");

FAQ

Common questions

Does signed checkout stop all triangulation fraud?+

No. It stops the first step: an agent paying a fake store. If a fraudster places an order by hand using a stolen card, that bypasses agent verification entirely — that's your existing fraud tools' job. But if the order came through an agent, ProveTheBuy catches it.

What if the recipient is a gift?+

Gifts are a legitimate mismatch. The policy lets you configure what counts as a match. When the recipient doesn't match but the approval is genuine — device-signed by the shopper — the order proceeds with a note in the evidence. No false blocks.

When does cross-store detection ship?+

Once several merchants are on ProveTheBuy Cloud. One store's data alone shows too little. We're onboarding merchants now — if you're interested, start with the Indie plan and your data will be ready when it launches.
